Sixteen more components read their spoken and visible strings from the `DelacourProvider` `labels` catalog and take a `labels` prop of their own: Slider, Sortable, SplitView, Splitter, StackCard, Steps, Support, Table, TagInput, Textarea, ThemeSelector, TimePicker, Timeline, Toast, Tour and Tree. Every default is today's English, so nothing changes until an app passes labels, with one exception: a TagInput commit that adds several tags now announces them in one sentence, "Added a, b", where it used to repeat the verb, "Added a, Added b". The per-string props these components already had (`accessibilityLabel`, `directionLabels`, `rejectMessages`, `removeAccessibilityLabel`, `clearAccessibilityLabel`, `placeholder`, `title`, `doneLabel`, `clearLabel` and the rest) still win over `labels`. The pure resolvers take the labels as a defaulted trailing argument (or a defaulted field of their options object), so existing calls keep their English. Composed sentences are whole functions — StackCard's throw, empty and undo announcements, Steps' and Timeline's spoken values, Support's spoken rows — so a language can reorder them. `TourLabels` and ThemeSelector's labels move into the catalog: Tour's keys are unchanged, and ThemeSelector's `labels` widens to `ThemeSelectorLabels` with a `group` name (`ThemeModeLabels` stays as a deprecated alias). `TableSortAccessibility.valueText` widens to `string`.
