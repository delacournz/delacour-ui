Two `Gesture.Pan()`s move a sheet: one on the `Handle`, one on the `Content`. Both are built from
the same handlers and both write the same value — the sheet's keyboard-free height — so a drag
that starts on the pill and a drag that starts on the copy feel the same.

```tsx
<BottomSheet
  enableContentPanningGesture
  enableHandlePanningGesture
  enableOverDrag
  enablePanDownToClose
  overDragResistanceFactor={2.5}
>
```

## The handle pan

Claims a touch immediately. The handle is the surface for dragging, so there is nothing under it a
tap could have meant.

```tsx
<BottomSheet.Handle enablePanningGesture={false} />
```

A per-part opt-out leaves the detector out for that handle alone; the root's
`enableHandlePanningGesture` switches every handle off.

## The content pan

Waits six points of vertical travel before it claims a touch and gives up after twelve horizontal.
A tap lands on the button or the field under it, and a horizontal pager inside the sheet keeps its
swipe. Both pans keep running when the finger leaves the view.

A scrollable body wraps its own native scroll in a gesture declared simultaneous with this pan;
[Scrollables](/docs/bottom-sheet/scrollables) is the rule for which of the two a finger moves.

## Over-drag

Past the highest snap point — and, when the sheet may not close, past the lowest — the sheet follows
the finger with a rubber band: slope one at the edge, square root beyond. `overDragResistanceFactor`
scales the travel; larger allows more. `enableOverDrag={false}` clamps instead.

The sheet never goes above the frame, however far the finger does.

## Pan down to close

With `enablePanDownToClose` (the default) the closed position is a candidate for the release, and a
drag below the lowest snap point runs toward it. Off, the lowest snap point is the floor and a drag below it
rubber-bands back. A [step](/docs/bottom-sheet/steps) marked `dismissible: false` switches it off
for as long as that step is current.

## The snap

A release picks the candidate nearest to where the finger was **heading**: the height it let go at
plus a fifth of a second of its velocity. A quick flick lands a snap point away; a slow drag lands on
the nearest one. Closed is a candidate only when the pan may close. On a tie the lower one wins.

The animation gets half the release velocity, in height space, so a spring continues the drag
rather than starting from rest.

<Callout type="warn">
The snap runs on `onFinalize`, not `onEnd`: a gesture that fails after activating never fires
`onEnd`, and the sheet would be left between snap points. If you compose a gesture of your own with the
pans, read the finalize.
</Callout>

## The keyboard, mid-drag

`enableBlurKeyboardOnGesture` (the default) dismisses the keyboard the moment a pan starts, and a
pan that ends heading down dismisses it regardless. Both go through keyboard-controller's
`KeyboardController.dismiss` on the JS thread.

## Haptics

The package imports nothing from a haptics library. Three props take a **worklet** and run it on
the UI thread, in the frame the crossing happened:

| Prop | Fires when |
| --- | --- |
| `onSnapPointHaptic` | a drag crossed a snap point |
| `onCloseHaptic` | a drag let go on a close |
| `onOverDragHaptic` | a drag left the snap point range and the rubber band took over — once per entry |

```tsx
import { Presets } from "react-native-pulsar";

<BottomSheet onCloseHaptic={Presets.System.impactLight} onSnapPointHaptic={Presets.System.selection}>
```

Pulsar's presets are worklets already; a function of your own needs the `"worklet"` directive and
may call nothing that is not one.

## Your own gesture

The pans are on the internal context as `pans.handle` and `pans.content`, ready for a
`GestureDetector` each. They are one shared object per pan and a gesture builder mutates in place,
so hand them to a detector untouched; a part that opts out leaves the detector out rather than
calling `.enabled(false)` on the shared object, which would switch it off for every other part
too.
