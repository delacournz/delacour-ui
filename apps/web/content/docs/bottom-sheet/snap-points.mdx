```tsx
<BottomSheet snapPoints={["40%", "85%"]} dynamicSizing={false}>
```

A **snap point** is a resting height: a height the sheet settles at when a drag lets go or a method
asks for it. The sheet is always at a snap point, moving between two, or closed. Closed is a position
the sheet can be in, but it is not a snap point — it has no index a method can name, and a sheet that
may not close never reaches it.

## Height space

Every number here is a **height**: pixels of sheet visible above its resting bottom line. `0` is
closed, snap points ascend, and the highest snap point is the largest number. There is no `translateY` in
the public surface; the engine derives it from a height at the moment a view needs it, and nowhere
else. That is why a detached card's snap points read the same as an attached sheet's even though the
card rests above the screen's bottom edge.

## Three kinds of snap point

| Written as | Resolves to |
| --- | --- |
| `240` | 240 pixels of sheet above the resting line |
| `"50%"` | half of the height **available** to the sheet — the frame's height above the resting line |
| the dynamic snap point | handle + content + footer content + the safe-area band, once |

`snapPoints` takes the first two. The third is added when `dynamicSizing` is on, which it is by
default, and it is the snap point a sheet with no `snapPoints` has. A `BottomSheet.ScrollView` feeds
it from its content size rather than its layout, which is what lets a list of forty rows size to
its rows and then scroll inside `maxDynamicContentSize`.

The list is normalised before anything reads it: ascending, unique, clamped to the available
height, invalid entries skipped. So `["85%", "40%"]` and `["40%", "85%"]` are the same sheet, and a
`"120%"` is the top of the frame.

```tsx
// One snap point, the content's own height, capped at 420.
<BottomSheet maxDynamicContentSize={420}>

// Two explicit snap points and the content's height, wherever it sorts among them.
<BottomSheet snapPoints={["30%", "90%"]}>

// Exactly two, whatever the content measures.
<BottomSheet snapPoints={["30%", "90%"]} dynamicSizing={false}>
```

<Callout type="warn">
The footer is **inside** the dynamic snap point. A sheet sized to its content with a sticky footer is
as tall as the handle, the content, the footer and the band — the footer never covers the last
line of content, and the buttons never sit on the home indicator. The library this replaces
excluded it, which is where most of its keyboard workarounds came from.
</Callout>

## Index

Snap points are named by their position in the sorted list: `0` is the lowest, `snapPoints.length - 1`
the highest, and `-1` is closed. `initialIndex` is where `open` lands; a controlled `index` snaps
an open sheet; `onIndexChange` reports every settle.

```tsx
<BottomSheet
  initialIndex={1}
  onIndexChange={(index, height, source) => console.log(index, height, source)}
  snapPoints={["30%", "60%", "90%"]}
>
```

The animated index — `animatedIndex` from `useBottomSheetAnimated()` — is continuous: `0.5` is
halfway between the first two snap points, `-0.5` halfway closed. It is interpolated from the
**keyboard-free** height, so a keyboard lifting the sheet never moves the index or darkens the
overlay; only the sheet moves.

`source` names why the settle happened: `mount`, `gesture`, `user`, `snapPoints`, `keyboard` or
`container`.

## Snap points move under a settled sheet

When the list changes while the sheet is open and idle — content grew, `snapPoints` changed — the
sheet animates to the same index in the new list. A container resize (a rotation) jumps instead: a
rotation is not something to animate through. Neither runs while a finger or an animation owns the
sheet.

## Insets

`topInset` is how much of the frame the sheet leaves clear at the top — the status bar, a header —
so `"100%"` never runs under it. `bottomInset` is the safe-area bottom: an attached sheet reserves
it under the content, a sticky footer stands on it, and a detached card floats above it. Pass
`useSafeAreaInsets()` in; the package reads nothing from `react-native-safe-area-context` itself.

```tsx
const insets = useSafeAreaInsets();

<BottomSheet bottomInset={insets.bottom} topInset={insets.top}>
```
